Your pulse follows the tap.

BeatSleep reads your heart on your Apple Watch, then taps your wrist just under your own rate. Your pulse follows the tap down, and you fall asleep.

There is no sound. There is nothing to watch. The screen exists only to prove it is working, and it fades away once you no longer need it.

How a night goes

  • Listening For the first 45 seconds nothing taps. BeatSleep measures the rate you are actually resting at, because the descent has to start where you are.
  • Entraining The cadence anchors to that rate and sheds four beats every ten minutes, staying just under your pulse — far enough to lead, close enough to be followed.
  • Holding Once you reach your floor the text decays away and the screen dims to a single slow orb. Nothing asks for your attention again.
  • Rising Inside your wake window the cadence inverts and quickens. You wake to a quickening, not to an alarm. No sound is ever played.

It follows you, not a schedule

If you turn over, if a noise wakes you, if your pulse climbs — BeatSleep stops pulling and re-anchors to your real heartbeat within a minute. Restlessness is followed, not fought.

Your floor is not a number you guess at. After three full nights BeatSleep derives it from your own overnight minimum, one beat at a time, and never leads you below it.

Nothing leaves your devices

BeatSleep has no account and no server. Your heart rate is read from HealthKit on the watch, the summary is computed there overnight, and it travels to your iPhone over Apple's encrypted device link. Optional iCloud sync writes to your own private iCloud database, in encrypted fields — never to us, because there is no us to send it to.

Read the privacy policy

Requires an Apple Watch. BeatSleep is not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition. If you have a sleep disorder or a heart condition, talk to a doctor rather than to an app.
